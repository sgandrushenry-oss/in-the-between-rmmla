What lives
around the talk.

This material is part of the larger scholarly project but does not all have to fit inside the ten-minute spoken presentation. Browse while the talk is happening or return later.

Liminality

A threshold condition of being between positions, identities, times, genres, or forms of recognition. In this project, liminality is multiple, moving, and unequal rather than a single bridge between stable shores.

Enjambment

In poetry, a sentence or phrase that continues beyond the end of a line. Here it becomes a way to think about lives that exceed institutional line breaks: semesters, milestones, categories, and imagined destinations.

State / a state

Not a claim that the university is literally a nation-state. State names both a condition and institutional acts of naming, classifying, evaluating, credentialing, and making subjects legible.

Epistemic rights

Beth Godbee's language for rights to knowledge, experience, or earned expertise—and for institutional responsibilities to affirm graduate writers as knowers.

Daemon / daimonic

Stefan Zweig's language for the “unrest that is in us all,” an unsettled pressure toward inquiry, transformation, danger, and excess. This project treats that unrest carefully rather than romanticizing suffering.

Conduction

A mentorship metaphor adapted from Zweig's lightning-conductor image: not replication and not abandonment, but creating conditions in which intellectual and creative energy can take form without predetermining the scholar who emerges.

Trajectory entity

Through Jay Lemke, Louise Wetherbee Phelps describes a human being as something understood across a lifespan trajectory rather than at one isolated instant.

Disciplinary lifecycling

Pinkert and Bowen's alternative to the linear academic career arc: movement, interruption, return, overlap, and change as ordinary rather than deficient forms of disciplinary life.

Notice

Notice before immediately judging. Attend to the question, source, sentence, resistance, or relation that keeps returning.

Wait

Remain open while continuing to do the work. Serendipity needs both preparation and room for the unexpected.

Accompany

Do not make graduate students inhabit uncertainty alone. Practice intellectual companionship and relational “power with.”

Mark

Recognize movement before graduation: changed arguments, returns after leave, resubmissions, pivots, and work that took longer than expected.

Care

Make intellectual risk materially possible through time, access, funding, rest, flexibility, reasonable workloads, and people who notice when systems become impossible.

Allow

Do not confuse finishing a requirement with finishing a person. Unfinishedness is part of intellectual life.

A scholarly performance
made as a website.

I wanted the form of the presentation to participate in its argument: the structure can be built in advance, but the version made by a particular room cannot be predetermined.

The visual material comes from my own photographs and artwork. The sailboat photographs were taken at Pineview Reservoir above Ogden, Utah, in the landscape where I will be presenting this work. The hand-worked boat image is my own oil-pastel/crayon drawing. I use these images as recurring materials rather than fixed symbols: shore, water, horizon, distance, movement, orientation, and return.

The site was built and revised using HTML5, CSS3, JavaScript, and a small Node.js server. Live participation uses Server-Sent Events, a JSON-backed contribution queue, presenter-controlled moderation, responsive browser layouts, and Git/GitHub versioning; the live deployment runs on Render.

This project developed through a mixed process of hand-coding, testing, visual design, and consultation. I also consulted my brother during development. AI-assisted coding and design tools were used selectively for prototyping, debugging, testing, and iteration. They functioned as development aids rather than as authors of the project. The argument, prose, photographs, artwork, visual direction, and final editorial choices are mine.

The audience contribution system asks for no names, emails, accounts, or demographic information. Contributions are reviewed before they appear publicly in the performance.

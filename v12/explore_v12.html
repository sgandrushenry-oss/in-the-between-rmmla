The spoken conference paper is intentionally smaller than the larger project. These sections hold the definitions, scholarship, pedagogical “little movements,” and making-process that remain available while the performance continues.

Definitions

Liminality. A threshold condition of being between positions, identities, times, genres, or forms of recognition; here, multiple, moving, and unequal rather than a single bridge between stable shores.

Enjambment. A poetic line break that does not end the grammatical sentence. In this project it becomes a figure for lives and learning that carry across institutional boundaries.

State / a state. A settled condition, and the institutional practices of naming, classifying, evaluating, credentialing, and making people legible.

Epistemic rights. Rights to knowledge, experience, or earned expertise; the project draws here on Beth Godbee.

Daemon / daimonic. Stefan Zweig's language for the unrest that presses beyond the familiar toward inquiry, while also carrying danger when it overwhelms.

Conduction. This project's mentoring metaphor for helping intensity move into inquiry, intuition into testing, experience into claim, and restlessness into craft without predetermining the scholar who must emerge.

Little Movements

NOTICE. Notice before immediately judging.

WAIT. Remain open while continuing to do the work.

ACCOMPANY. Do not make graduate students inhabit uncertainty alone.

MARK. Recognize movement before graduation.

CARE. Make intellectual risk materially possible.

ALLOW. Do not confuse finishing a requirement with finishing a person.

These practices remain part of the larger graduate-education project even though the conference talk now centers the poet/state/enjambment argument.

About the Project

In the Between is a scholarly performance whose medium is a live website rather than a conventional slide deck. Samantha Andrus-Henry wrote the talk, developed the argument and visual concept, and created the artwork and photographs used throughout the presentation.

The photographs were taken by Andrus-Henry at Pineview Reservoir above Ogden, Utah, the landscape in which this RMMLA presentation is being delivered. The hand-worked boat image is her own oil-pastel/crayon drawing. Rather than treating these images as decoration, the project uses shore, water, sail, distance, interruption, and handmade marks as recurring materials for thinking about liminality and becoming.

The live project uses HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and a small Node.js server. Server-Sent Events allow approved audience fragments to move into the projected presentation in real time; a separate moderation interface keeps contributions from appearing publicly until reviewed.

The development process also included consultation with Andrus-Henry's brother and selective use of generative AI tools as a development aid for prototyping, debugging, code revision, and interface testing. AI was one tool among several in the making process; the argument, scholarship, writing, aesthetic direction, photographs, artwork, source choices, and final editorial/design decisions remain Andrus-Henry's.

The project is intentionally transparent about its making without making the toolchain the subject of the performance.
